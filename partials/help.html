The Fundamental Score — 9 factors, step by step

  • Every stock is scored on nine factors in three categories.
  • The factor weights add up to exactly 10, so the Overall Score is simply the sum of the nine factor scores.
  • Each factor looks at several screener.in fields, to reduce single-year noise:
    • Financial Health: adds Altman Z Score, Current Ratio and Debtor Days.
    • Profitability: blends this year's ROCE/ROE 50/50 with the long-run average.
    • Growth: adds a cash-flow check (Free Cash Flow and Operating Cash Flow).
    • Valuation Metrics: compares P/E with the stock's own 5-year P/E.
    • Governance: adds a small adjustment for FII/DII holding change.
    • Capital Reinvestment: uses CROIC, falling back to ROIC if CROIC is missing.
  • Contingent Liabilities, Graham Number and NCAVPS appear next to the relevant factor as cross-checks. They are not part of the score.

Step 2 — score each factor from 0 up to its own weight

  • Each factor is scored on its own scale, e.g. Financial Health runs from 0 to 1.8.
  • Full weight means top marks on that dimension; 0 means it failed the check entirely.
  • If one input metric is missing, the factor is scored on the metrics that are present.

Step 3 — add up the nine factor scores

Overall Score = Financial Health + Profitability + Growth + Valuation Metrics + Moat + Governance + Earnings Predictability + Capital Reinvestment Efficiency + Margin of Safety

  • The weights already sum to 10, so the total lands on a 0–10 scale with no rescaling.

Step 4 — roll factors up into three Subscores

  • Each stock also shows a 0–10 Subscore per category.
  • A solid Overall Score can hide one weak category; the split makes that visible.
SubscoreBuilt fromFormula
Traditional SubscoreFinancial Health, Profitability, Growth, Valuation Metrics(sum of those 4 factor scores) ÷ 5.85 × 10
Buffett SubscoreMoat, Governance, Earnings Predictability, Capital Reinvestment Efficiency(sum of those 4 factor scores) ÷ 3.15 × 10
Valuation SubscoreMargin of Safety (alone)(that factor's score) ÷ 1.0 × 10
  • 5.85 and 3.15 are just each category's own weights added up (1.8+1.8+1.125+1.125 and 0.9+0.9+0.675+0.675).

Where the data comes from

  • Source: screener.in, cross-checked against filings and third-party research (Simply Wall St, TipRanks, Equitymaster, CARE Ratings, MoneyWorks4Me).
  • Peer ranking: most factors rank a stock's metrics as a percentile against its exact peers, not against fixed thresholds.
    • Peers are stocks in the same Industry Group and the same industry, e.g. Commodity Chemicals within Chemicals & Petrochemicals.
    • If that industry has fewer than 10 stocks, the comparison widens to the Industry Group, then to the whole ~2,000-stock universe.
    • The Industry Peers panel at the bottom of each stock lists the exact peers.
  • Margin of Safety is the exception: it is always ranked against the entire dataset, since a bargain is a bargain market-wide.
  • Intrinsic Value is Screener.in's own published figure, the single source of truth used everywhere in this app.
    • It is a growth/ROCE-adjusted Graham Number: √(EPS × Book Value × Max P/E × Max P/BV).
    • Max P/E = 5-year median sales growth × 1.5 (capped 8–100). Max P/BV = 5-year ROCE ÷ 8 (capped 1–10).
    • It is not a full discounted cash flow model, and Screener calls it "a very subjective ratio".
  • Cross-checks: Graham Number and NCAVPS come straight from the screener.in export. A big gap from Intrinsic Value, or a price below NCAVPS, is flagged on the factor card but never changes the score.
  • Verify it yourself: each stock shows a "Last verified" date, a screener.in link and a TradingView chart link.

The Technical Score — how it's calculated

  • A single 0–10 score for the stock's price momentum, built from five components.
  • It uses MACD(12,26,9) and RSI(14) on the monthly and weekly charts, plus a valuation input.
  • It's plain arithmetic on TradingView price, MACD and RSI readings. There is no research or judgment involved, and no buy/sell labels.
  • Tech 2 and Tech 3 (two extra scores tried earlier) are hidden on every screen; this is the only technical score shown.

  • Each timeframe is scored by the zone its RSI falls in:

Step 4 — add them up

Technical Score = Monthly MACD + Weekly MACD + Monthly RSI + Weekly RSI + Valuation points

  • The result is rounded to 2 decimals.
  • A Live Data refresh scores finished weeks and months only. The week or month still in progress is shown on the stock page as Provisional and does not change the saved score.
  • The five maximums add up to 10, so no rescaling is needed when every component is present.

When data is missing

  • TradingView sometimes leaves a component blank, often the monthly figures for newer stocks. That component is left out of the sum.
  • The score is then rescaled to 0–10 over the components that are present.
    • Example: weekly MACD + weekly RSI + Valuation have a combined weight of 1.5 + 2.0 + 2.0 = 5.5.
    • The score is then (points earned ÷ 5.5) × 10.
  • The stock's data-completeness badge (e.g. "3/5 signals") shows how many components were available.
  • If TradingView has no reading for a stock, its previous Technical Score is kept unchanged.
  • The Technical Score is refreshed with Refresh all stocks in the same section, straight from TradingView. One click takes the price, MACD and RSI of every stock (including the week or month still in progress), downloads the full price history for stocks that have none so they get the stricter completed-weeks score, and adds the newly finished weekly or monthly bars for the rest. Scores of stocks with a fresh completed-weeks score are left as they are.

Where the data comes from

  • TradingView, through the Refresh all stocks button on the Data Refresh page. Updates are manual.
  • Each refresh also updates the stock's current price, and with it the Margin of Safety.
  • More detail is in Knowledge Base §4.

Price charts and the MACD panel

  • Each stock page shows a Weekly and a Monthly candlestick chart with volume bars underneath.
  • Green candles closed above their open; red candles closed below it.
  • Below the volume there is a MACD panel: a blue MACD line, an orange signal line, and bars for the gap between them.
  • Blue bars mean MACD is above its signal line (bullish); orange bars mean it is below (bearish).
  • The dashed line is zero.
  • The first 33 bars are faded and dashed, labelled "warm-up: less reliable", because MACD needs about 34 bars to settle.
  • A stock with fewer than 35 bars has no MACD panel.
  • Hover or touch a bar to see its prices, MACD, signal and gap.
  • Press Legend to see what each colour means.
  • The panel is only a picture; it never changes a score.

Getting a new version of the dashboard

  • The version number is shown at the top of the left menu.
  • When a new version is published, an open page reloads itself once.
  • It waits until nothing is running (a refresh or an import) and you are not typing.
  • If the new version does not arrive, a "Reload now" button appears at the bottom.

Glossary

Quick definitions for the less obvious terms.

Altman Z Score
A bankruptcy-risk score. Higher is safer; low flags financial-distress risk.
Capital Reinvestment Efficiency
When earnings are retained, are they reinvested at a strong return, or left idle?
CROIC (Cash Return on Invested Capital)
Free cash flow as a % of invested capital. Cash-based, so harder to flatter than ROIC. Preferred in Capital Reinvestment.
Current Ratio
Current assets ÷ current liabilities. Can the company cover near-term bills?
D/E (Debt-to-Equity)
Total debt ÷ equity. Lower means less reliance on borrowing.
Earnings Predictability
How steady profit growth has been. A smooth compounder scores higher than an erratic one.
FII / DII Holding Change
3-year change in foreign and domestic institutional ownership. Shows whether big investors are accumulating or exiting. Small, capped effect on Governance.
Factors badge (for example 5/9)
Next to the Fundamental Score: how many of the nine factors had usable data. A note such as "4 researched, data unavailable" means those factors were researched but the figures could not be found.
Free Cash Flow
Cash left after capital spending. Real cash, not accounting profit.
Graham Number
Benjamin Graham's fair value: √(22.5 × EPS × Book Value). Shown as a cross-check, not scored.
Incomplete data (tag)
Appears on a factor card when some of its figures are missing. The factor is scored on whatever was available. Refresh Fundamental Info tries to fill the gaps.
Interest Coverage
Operating profit ÷ interest expense. How many times over the interest bill is covered.
Intrinsic Value (Screener.in)
Screener.in's own fair-value estimate, used for Margin of Safety, the MoS Rating filter and the Analysis section. A growth/ROCE-adjusted Graham Number, so treat it as one reasoned estimate, not a guarantee.
Margin of Safety
(Intrinsic Value − Current Price) ÷ Intrinsic Value. Positive = trades below intrinsic value; negative = above. Scored as a percentile against all stocks.
Moat
A durable advantage (brand, scale, niche) that protects profits from competitors.
NCAVPS (Net Current Asset Value Per Share)
(Current assets − total liabilities) ÷ shares. Graham's "liquidation value". A price below it is a deep-value "net-net" signal, shown as a flag.
P/B (Price-to-Book)
Share price ÷ book value per share. Below 1 can mean cheap relative to net assets.
P/E (Price-to-Earnings)
Share price ÷ earnings per share. Higher means more growth expected, or an expensive stock.
Piotroski F-Score
A 0–9 checklist of profitability, leverage and efficiency signals. Higher means a stronger, improving business. Proxy for Governance.
Pledged Shares
Promoter shares used as loan collateral. High pledging is a governance risk.
Promoter Holding
% owned by the founders/promoters. A falling trend is worth watching.
Quick Ratio
(Current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities. A stricter liquidity test than the current ratio.
Researched (tag)
The card was refreshed with company-specific research (plain-language bullets and sources) by Refresh Fundamental Info. The date of the last refresh is shown in the Live Data Refresh section, not on the card.
F and T badges
Next to a stock name in a list: F is how old its fundamentals are, T how old its technicals. Green is fresh, amber is ageing, red is stale and dashed means never refreshed. Hover for the exact age. Use the Fundamental and Technical buttons above the list (or tick rows) to refresh them; a refresh is saved and continues when you reopen the dashboard.
ROCE (Return on Capital Employed)
Operating profit as a % of total capital (equity + debt). How efficiently all capital is used.
ROE (Return on Equity)
Net profit as a % of shareholders' equity. How well owners' money is turned into profit.
ROIC (Return on Invested Capital)
Operating profit as a % of invested capital. Used only when CROIC is missing.
Subscore
A 0–10 score for one category (Traditional, Buffett or Valuation), so categories can be compared side by side.

This is not investment advice — it's a structured way to organize public data for your own research.
